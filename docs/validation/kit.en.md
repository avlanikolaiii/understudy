# Validation kit (English)

Nothing here has been sent. Don't send outreach until it's approved.

## What the interviews can and can't tell us

- **Pilot gate:** if at least 5 of 10 people send a recurring client update that takes them 1 hour or more a week, and at least 3 agree to share anonymized examples, offer a **disclosed manual pilot**.
- **This is not proof of demand.** It measures pain and willingness to participate. Willingness to pay is measured separately: does anyone accept a concrete paid offer once its scope and price are approved?

## 1 · Outreach message

> Hi [name], I'm building a Mac tool for agencies that learns recurring client work by watching you do it once, starting with the weekly client update.
>
> Nothing is for sale. I'm trying to learn how agencies actually do this job before I build too much. Would you give me 20 minutes to walk me through how your team put together last week's client updates?
>
> If it helps, I'll send you a short summary of what I learn across agencies.
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]

## 2 · Interview script (20 minutes)

Ask about what they actually did. Don't pitch, and don't ask whether they'd use it.

**Their last real update (~8 min)**
1. Walk me through the last client update you sent. Where did you start?
2. Which apps and files did you open, in what order?
3. How long did it take, from first click to sending?
4. How many clients get an update like this, and how often?
5. What changes from one client to another? What stays the same?

**Where it goes wrong (~6 min)**
6. Tell me about the last time an update was late, wrong, or missing a number. What happened?
7. Who checks the update before it goes out? What do they look for?
8. Have you tried to automate or delegate any of this? What happened?

**Value (~4 min)**
9. If this happened without you, what would you do with that time?
10. What do you pay for today to save time on client work?
11. What would you need to see before you trusted a tool to prepare these?

**Close (~2 min)**
12. Could you share one or two past updates, anonymized, so we can build against real examples? (Send the permission wording first.)
13. Who else should I talk to?

## 3 · Permission to use example reports

> Thanks for offering to share examples. Here's exactly how we'd use them:
>
> • What we're asking for: one or two past weekly client updates, and the spreadsheet or data they were built from, if possible.
> • Please remove or replace client names, contact details, and anything confidential first. Changing the real numbers is fine too. We care about the structure.
> • How we'll use them: only to design and test Understudy's first workflow. We won't share them outside our small team, publish them, or use them to contact your clients.
> • Your client's permission: if your contract with the client restricts sharing their data, even anonymized, please check with them first or don't share.
> • Deleting them: tell us at any time and we'll delete everything you sent and confirm by email.
>
> If that works for you, just reply "Yes, you can use them as described."

Examples received are stored in `client-examples/`, which git ignores and which never leaves this Mac.

## 4 · What to record after each conversation

Role and company type · clients receiving updates and how often · time per update · sources and tools · what varies by client · last thing that went wrong · current spend to save time · examples shared (with permission on file: yes/no).

## 5 · Disclosed manual pilot (only after the pilot gate)

Prepare 2–3 agencies' weekly updates by hand for a few weeks, **telling them clearly that a person is doing it**. Never present manual work as Understudy running. The pilot's scope and price need approval before anyone is offered it.
